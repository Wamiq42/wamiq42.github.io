Echo Room VR In development

Genre: Echolocation puzzle Platform: Meta Quest Engine: Unity · OpenXR · XR Interaction Toolkit
Shaders Spatial Audio Mic Input VR Interaction Game Design

The idea

You are in a pitch-black room. You can't see — but you can make sound. Press a button, snap, or shout into the mic, and a pulse of sound ripples outward, briefly lighting up the walls, edges, and objects it touches before everything fades back to darkness. You explore, remember what you saw, find the hidden switches, and open the door to the next room.

Play blind. See with sound.

PING · REVEAL · REMEMBER

In a game about darkness, screenshots don't say much — this is the game's actual color language: a sonar pulse on the void. Footage coming once the first rooms are polished.

Design pillars

  • Darkness is the default. The world is hidden until you reveal it — light is a reward for acting, never free.
  • Sound is sight. Every reveal is tied to a sound the player or world made. The fantasy is echolocation.
  • Calm, eerie, readable. Tense and atmospheric, not horror — and the important things are always readable when revealed.

What's built so far

The hardest, most differentiating part — the echolocation sensory loop — is working:

  • Physically accurate echoes. Pings reflect off geometry and return as spatial 3D audio, delayed by distance ÷ 343 m/s — the real speed of sound. Far walls genuinely answer later.
  • Sonar reveal shader. A custom shader sweeps a teal pulse across surfaces the ping touches, then fades them back into the void.
  • Shout to ping. Microphone input triggers pings — snap your fingers or call out, and the room answers.
  • VR interaction & puzzles. Physical button presses, door logic, and a puzzle framework binding them together — the first room type (logic rooms) is playable.
  • Architecture built to grow. Interface-driven design (input sources, echo interactables, puzzle elements) and ScriptableObject-driven level data, so new rooms and mechanics slot in without rewrites.

What's next

  • Maze rooms — blueprints are designed; exploration by echo is the next playable layer.
  • Echo materials — pitch and volume varying by surface and size, and echo-absorbing materials.
  • More room types — pattern rooms (memorize and mimic sound sequences) and stealth rooms.
  • Comfort & polish — teleport locomotion, haptics, and an in-VR radial menu.
